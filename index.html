The Word We Keep Saying

Everyone wants more leadership. Almost no one means the same thing by it.

Mara OkaforOct 21, 2026 · 6 min read
♡ 12824 commentsShare

Last month I sat in three meetings where someone said the team needed "more leadership." Each time, the room nodded. Each time, I suspect, every person in it pictured something different.

It's one of those words that does a lot of work without anyone checking what the work is. We put it in job descriptions, performance reviews and award speeches. We rarely stop to ask what we're actually asking for.

A word with many jobs

Sometimes leadership means authority: the person whose name sits at the top of the chart. Sometimes it means courage, the willingness to say the unpopular thing first. Sometimes it’s shorthand for intuition. We call someone a natural leader when they seem to know which way to go before the rest of us have finished reading the map.

That last meaning is the slipperiest. Intuition looks like magic from the outside. From the inside, it's usually years of small observations that have quietly added up.

When we praise intuition without naming it, we make leadership sound like something you're born with. That's convenient for the people who already have it, and discouraging for everyone else.

What the good ones actually did

I asked a dozen friends to describe the best leader they had ever worked for. Nobody mentioned a vision statement. Nobody mentioned a speech.

They talked about a manager who remembered that their kid had an exam that week. A founder who asked the quietest person in the room what she thought, and then waited. A teacher who wrote two careful lines on every single essay.

The leaders people remember rarely said the most. They were the ones who noticed.

Noticing isn't glamorous. It doesn't fit on a slide. But it can be learned, which is more than we can say for charisma.

Saying what we mean

So here's a small proposal. The next time you're about to say a team needs more leadership, try finishing the sentence. More leadership, meaning what? Clearer decisions? Someone willing to take the blame? Someone who notices when things are quietly going wrong?

The answer will tell you far more than the word ever could. It might even turn out to be something you can start doing yourself, tomorrow, without anyone's permission.

Thanks for reading Field Notes on Work. If this was useful, pass it to someone who says "leadership" a lot.
